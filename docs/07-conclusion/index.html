07 · Conclusion

What This Course Covered — and Where It Stops

The pipeline, end to end

Starting from three raw data sources — gigapixel pathology slides, multiparametric MRI, and structured clinical records — this course built a complete multimodal pipeline: tissue-aware patch extraction and per-modality preprocessing, foundation-model encoders for imaging plus a fixed-vector clinical encoder, three architecturally distinct fusion strategies sharing the same pooling and head, a censoring-aware survival objective trained alongside ordinary binary classification, slide-to-patient aggregation at inference time, and evaluation through both numbers (AUROC, C-index) and visual evidence (Kaplan-Meier stratification, attention heatmaps).

Key takeaways

  • Fusion strategy is a real, measurable design choice — early, intermediate, and late fusion make different assumptions about where modalities should interact, and holding pooling/head capacity constant across all three isolates that choice from everything else.
  • Multi-instance data doesn't need multi-instance-aware models. Handling a patient's variable number of WSI slides as independent training rows, then aggregating predictions post-hoc, meant the fusion architectures themselves stayed simple.
  • Censoring is information, not missing data. The discrete-time negative log-likelihood loss uses censored patients' partial "survived at least this long" signal directly, rather than discarding them.
  • A single risk score isn't the end product. Turning it into quantile-based risk groups, and pairing that with visualization — attention heatmaps, KM curves — is what makes a number something a clinician (or a reviewer) can actually evaluate.

Limitations

  • Cohort size. ~95 patients is small for deep multimodal fusion; every architecture choice in this course (e.g. intermediate fusion's 256-dim shared space instead of a much larger one) was made to keep parameter counts comparable and avoid overfitting on this scale — a larger cohort would change those trade-offs.
  • No layer-wise feature aggregation. Both imaging encoders use only their final transformer block's embedding. Aggregating multiple intermediate layers can capture complementary information a single layer misses, at the cost of extra complexity this course deliberately avoided for clarity.
  • Fixed decision thresholds and quantile splits. 0.5 for classification and a 20% quantile for risk stratification are reasonable defaults, not tuned choices — a deployed system would calibrate both against a held-out validation set.
  • Single-institution data. All patients come from one challenge cohort; generalization to other scanners, staining protocols, or patient populations is untested.

Where to go next

  • Try layer-wise feature aggregation for one encoder and compare against the single-layer baseline used throughout this course.
  • Tune the classification threshold and risk-stratification quantile against a validation split instead of using fixed defaults.
  • Extend to more modalities, or additional CHIMERA tasks, using the same fusion-comparison structure.
